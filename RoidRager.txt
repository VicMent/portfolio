1. General Concept
1.1 The Idea in One Paragraph
Roid Rager is a first-person wave survival game where you play as a low life trying to avoid paying taxes by becoming the most overpowered, chemically enhanced, surgically modified fighter in the city. Every run you collect cards that represent body modifications such as steroids, drugs, cybernetics, surgery and use them to fight off increasingly dangerous waves of enemies with your physics based stretchy fists. Between waves you spin a slot machine and visit a black market dealer to get stronger. You die, you expand or edit your deck and start over.



1.2 Genre Mix
This game combines four genres in one:
Roguelike: Every run is different. You die, you start from scratch. Permanent upgrades are limited to meta progression unlocked after each run.
Deckbuilding: Your power comes from building a deck of body mod cards. Weapon cards, ability cards and upgrade cards all slot into your loadout.
Wave Survival: You fight wave after wave of enemies in an arena. Each wave gets harder. Survive as long as you can.
FPS Physics Combat: You fight using first-person physics-based hands. You can punch, pull and throw enemies and objects. Movement and combat feel physical and reactive.

1.3 What Makes It Unique
There is no other game that combines all of these things at once:
Physics-based stretchy FPS hands as the main combat tool
A slot machine as the upgrade screen between waves
The body modification theme as the core progression metaphor
A deliberately offensive, anti-moral tone (tax evasion as the hero motivation)
PS2 aesthetic in a modern game engine


2. Story & Worldbuilding
2.1 Setting
The game takes place in a fictional shithole city,  a rotting urban hellscape somewhere between a Vegas strip knockoff and a crumbling Eastern European ghetto. Neon signs, filth, drug dealers on corners, casinos, and a government that takes half of your beer money to then tax you on beers 🙁.
The city acts as one big arena for street fights. This is where the entire game will take place.

2.2 The Player Character
You play as an unnamed low life that is sick of paying taxes and abiding by social norms. No real backstory is given. You are broke, chemically enhanced, surgically modified, and extremely motivated to avoid paying taxes. That is your entire character arc. This mindset was heavily inspired by the movie “New Kids Turbo”.
 
As you get stronger through body modifications, your character's arms visually change, new limbs, cybernetic implants, steroid-bloated muscles. This is expressed through the card system, not cutscenes.

★ Nice to have: Switching between different arm meshes that are more muscular and potentially using blend shapes to gradually grow the muscle mesh while playing..


2.3 Narrative Structure
There is no traditional story. The worldbuilding is expressed through enemy design, environment details, the items you can buy, and the general vibe of the game. The world speaks for itself.


The motivation is clear: evade taxes. The government sends increasingly dangerous agents to stop you.
A future boss encounter with a SWAT commander or government agent is planned, it represents the state trying to collect your taxes. This is a nice to have not MVP.
Victory = getting a high score in the form of waves survived. Defeat = getting killed. There is no moral resolution your character is willing to settle for.

3. Visual Style
3.1 Overall Direction
The art style is deliberately retro PS2-era 3D. Low poly models, pixelated textures, photo projection textures (real photos mapped onto simple geometry like in early PS2 games). This style is chosen for three reasons:
It makes the project more achievable for this one man army.
It fits the seedy, low budget atmosphere of the game world perfectly
It creates a unique visual identity and has a growing fanbase in the modern indie space

3.2 Technical Style Details
Element
Visual Target
Geometry
Low-poly, hard-edged models (500–2000 poly per character)
Textures
Low resolution (128x128 to 512x512), photo-projected or hand-painted
Lighting
Flat or minimal real-time lighting, heavy use of baked light
Post Processing
Scanlines, slight CRT warp, optional film grain
Color palette
Dirty neon oranges, deep reds, dark greens, sickly yellows
UI
Chunky pixel font, VHS-style overlays, glitchy text effects


3.3 Reference Games
The following existing games serve as visual reference points:
Megabonk: Low poly retro look that is perfect for optimization with big waves of enemies.
Grandpa High On Retro: Flat, simple visuals that still communicate extremely well.
Duke Nukem 3d: Flat, classic dark look.
Clover Pit: Very close to the style and feel i want to achieve.

3.4 Feasibility Assessment
This style is very achievable. Low-poly modeling is faster than high-poly. Photo textures can be found freely or taken yourself. Post-processing shaders are easy in Unreal Engine. This is deliberately a style that one person should be able to pull off.

4. Core Gameplay Mechanics
IMPORTANT: In this section, MVP means mechanics that will be built during the course. 'Nice to have' means mechanics that are planned for the full game but will NOT be built during the prototype phase.


4.1 Physics-Based FPS Combat
✔ MVP: This is the single most important mechanic in the game. Everything else supports this.


You fight entirely with your hands. Your arms are slightly stretchy and elastic, allowing for satisfying punching at short to long range depending on your upgrades. The system works like this:
Punch: Press left/right mouse button to throw a punch. Physics force is applied to enemies on contact. Enemies ragdoll or explode when dead.
Pull: Hold a button and grab an enemy or object toward you.
Throw: Grab an object or enemy and send it flying. Can use enemies as weapons against other enemies.

Implementation approach: Use Unreal Engine's physics system using PhysicsAssets and constraints on the arm. Apply impulse forces on hit contact. Enemy characters use Skeletal Meshes with Physics Assets for ragdoll setups. Limb destruction is handled by switching from an animated skeleton to a simulated Physics Asset with detached parts on death.
★ Nice to have: Dragging enemies along the floor as a movement option — cool but complex to implement cleanly.


4.2 The Card System (Body Modifications)
✔ MVP: Core deckbuilding loop. Three card types: Weapon, Upgrade, Ability.


Your loadout is a small deck of cards. Each card represents a body modification you have applied to yourself. There are three types:
Card Type
What It Does
Example
Weapon Card
Replaces or augments your hand attack
Knuckle Duster, Glock
Upgrade Card
Passive stat boost or body mod
Steroid Injection, Cybernetic Arm, Extra Kidney
Ability Card
Active ability triggered manually
Adrenaline Spike, Fentanyl Rush, Double Jump Legs


Cards are collected between waves via the slot machine or the black market dealer. You keep cards for the entire run. At the start of a new run, you pick a starting loadout from a small set of options.
Implementation: Cards are Data Assets in Unreal Engine. Each card has a type, a stat modifier or effect trigger, a name and texture. The player's loadout is an array of active card data. Passive cards are applied at wave start. Active cards are triggered by a bound input action.

4.3 The Heart Rate Mana System
✔ MVP: Mana is your heart rate. Using abilities raises it. Going over the limit damages you.


Instead of a traditional mana bar, the game uses your character's heart rate as the resource system:
Normal resting heart rate = 70 BPM. This is shown on screen as a heartbeat monitor UI element.
Using an ability or drug raises your BPM.
If your BPM goes above 150, you start taking damage from cardiac stress.
BPM naturally decreases back to baseline over time if you stop using abilities.
Some cards let you raise your BPM limit or reduce damage taken over the limit.


This system rewards aggressive play (use abilities, fight fast) but punishes overuse without management. It also fits the drug-and-stimulant theme of the game perfectly.
Implementation: A float variable tracks current BPM. Abilities increase it by a set amount per use. A passive decay function reduces it over time. A damage-over-time function triggers when it exceeds the threshold.
★ Nice to have: Drug abilities that kick in with a delay,  you take the drug mid-fight and the effect comes in 5-10 seconds later. More realistic and fun but complex to balance cleanly in MVP.


4.4 Wave Survival Structure
✔ MVP: Enemies spawn in waves. Each wave is harder. Survive 3-5 waves. Boss is a future addition.


The full run structure per encounter:
Wave spawns —> enemies come from edges of the arena
Fight —> use cards, manage BPM, survive
Wave cleared —> brief pause, loot drops
Upgrade time —> go to the slot machine and/or shop
Repeat for next wave
Survive as many waves as possible


4.5 The Slot Machine Upgrade System
✔ MVP: After each wave, a slot machine appears. Spin it to get a random card or upgrade.


The slot machine is the primary reward mechanism between waves. It works like this:
Three reels spin and land on symbols. Each symbol represents a card type or special outcome.
Matching three of the same gives you the best version of that reward (rare card, big upgrade).
Two matching symbols give a standard reward. No match gives a small consolation prize (currency or a common card).
You can spend currency ('Guap') to get extra spins.



This system introduces randomness and gambling energy that fits the game's chaotic, hedonistic theme. Implementation: A UI-based animation system for the reels. The outcome is determined at the moment of pressing spin using weighted random selection. Card rewards are pulled from a pool that scales with current wave number.
★ Nice to have: Investing Guap in meme coins (Pepe Coin etc.) for high-risk high-reward meta progression  very funny concept but fully post-MVP.


4.6 Black Market Shop
✔ MVP: A dealer appears optionally between waves. Spend currency to buy specific cards.


The black market gives you player agency unlike the slot machine (pure RNG), here you can choose what to buy from a small selection of 3-4 items. Prices are high. Currency is earned from kills and wave completion bonuses.
Implementation: A simple shop UI that populates with randomly selected cards from a curated pool. Cards are removed from the pool once bought to avoid duplicates. Shop inventory resets between waves.

5.3 Platform Choice
The game is built for PC (Windows) as the primary platform for the following reasons:
The abilities work best with mouse and keyboard
Roguelike deckbuilders have their core audience on PC
Unreal Engine deployment to PC is straightforward and well documented
No certification or submission process required for early prototype testing

★ Nice to have: A future console release (PS5 or Switch) is possible but would require controller remapping for the physics hands, which is a non-trivial redesign.



6. Game Design Overview
6.1 Rules of the Game
Before you start each run you select wich upgrade cards you want from a pool of unlocked cards.
Each wave consists of multiple small waves of enemies constantly spawning.
You have a health bar. If it hits 0, you die and the run ends (permadeath).
Your heart rate (BPM) is your mana. Going over the limit hurts you.
After each wave you get an upgrade via the slot machine. Optionally visit the shop.
After 3-5 waves, if you are still alive you win the run but you can keep going. A boss fight is planned for a future version.
Surviving all waves wins the run and unlocks a small meta progression reward.
On death or victory, all cards are lost. You start fresh next run.

6.2 Win and Loss Conditions
Condition
Details
WIN
Survive all waves in the run (boss fight planned as a future nice-to-have) (you can keep going)
LOSS
Player health reaches zero -> permanent death, run ends
META WIN
Unlock new starting cards or cosmetics via meta progression after winning a run


6.3 Level Design
✔ MVP: One polished arena: The City Map. One map for the entire MVP.


The arena is a single enclosed urban environment, think a fenced off plaza in a decaying city. Key design priorities:
Open enough for enemies to approach from multiple angles, but enclosed enough to feel claustrophobic and intense
Some obstacles and cover to break up the space and create tactical moments
Visual layering  the slot machine and shop are physical objects in the world available between waves
Dirty neon aesthetic, broken signs, garbage, flickering lights

The arena does not change between waves in the MVP. Enemy spawn points are varied to keep each wave feeling different. Enemy count and composition scale with wave number and have randomness attached to them so every run is unique.
★ Nice to have: Multiple arenas / maps for future versions: Vegas strip environment, underground casino, government building raid.


6.4 Progression Loop Summary
The core loop looks like this:
1. Pick starting loadout
2. Fight wave → kill enemies → earn Guap
3. Spin slot machine → get card reward
4. Optional: visit black market with Guap
5. Next wave (repeat 2-4 for 3-5 waves)
6. Survive all waves → run complete or keep going
7. Win/Lose → meta progression → new run

7.3 Physics Constraints
The physics-based hand system is the most technically demanding part of the game. Key constraints:
Maximum arm reach distance: 3-4 meters. Beyond this the grab fails and no physics is applied.
Enemy ragdolls are disabled during wave fights and enabled on death to save performance.
Limb separation on death is handled by swapping the animated model for a pre-broken version with Rigidbody components on each part.
Maximum simultaneous ragdolls: 8. Older ragdolls are replaced by static meshes if the limit is exceeded.

7.4 Card System Technical Notes
Cards are Unreal Data Assets with fields for: card name, card type enum, description, texture, stat modifiers, and a linked Blueprint function for the effect.
The player loadout is stored as a List<CardData> on a PlayerManager singleton.
Passive cards are applied once at wave start. Active cards listen for a button input event.
Card pool per run is generated at run start from a master card database, filtered by unlock status from meta progression data.

7.5 Memory & File Size Guidelines
All textures compressed to DXT1/DXT5 in the Unreal Engine packaging settings.
Audio clips using LoadType.CompressedInMemory for short sound effects.
Enemies use Unreal's built-in object pooling via Actor pooling to avoid frequent spawn/destroy calls.
Scene assets stay under 50MB per scene. No single texture over 2MB.
